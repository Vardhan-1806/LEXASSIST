LEXASSIST LEGAL KNOWLEDGE BASE – Indian Law Basics
=====================================================

## BAIL LAW (CrPC)
Bail is the temporary release of an accused person awaiting trial, in exchange for security or surety.

Bailable Offences (Section 436 CrPC): Bail is a matter of right for bailable offences. Police or court must grant bail.

Non-Bailable Offences (Section 437 CrPC): Bail is at the court's discretion. Factors considered:
- Nature and gravity of the accusation
- Antecedents of the accused
- Possibility of fleeing from justice
- Likelihood of tampering with evidence

Anticipatory Bail (Section 438 CrPC): A person can apply for bail in anticipation of arrest. Filed in Sessions Court or High Court.

Bail in High Court/Supreme Court: If lower courts deny bail, higher courts can be approached. Supreme Court grants bail under Article 32 and Section 439 CrPC.

## FIR (FIRST INFORMATION REPORT)
An FIR is a written document prepared by police when they receive information about a cognizable offence under Section 154 CrPC.

How to file FIR:
1. Visit the nearest police station
2. Give information to the Station House Officer (SHO)
3. Police must register FIR for cognizable offences
4. If police refuse, approach the Superintendent of Police or file a complaint before a Judicial Magistrate under Section 156(3) CrPC
5. You can also send a registered letter to SP/DSP if police refuse to register FIR

Zero FIR: Can be filed at any police station regardless of jurisdiction. Must be transferred to the appropriate police station.

## DIVORCE LAW (Hindu Marriage Act 1955)
Mutual Consent Divorce (Section 13B): 
- Both parties must agree
- Must have lived separately for at least 1 year
- First motion petition filed
- 6 months cooling period (court may waive in some cases)
- Second motion filed to confirm divorce
- Decree granted by Family Court

Contested Divorce (Section 13): Grounds include:
- Cruelty (mental or physical)
- Desertion for 2 or more years
- Conversion to another religion
- Unsound mind / mental disorder
- Leprosy or venereal disease
- Renunciation of the world
- Presumed dead (7 years missing)

For Muslim couples: Divorce governed by Muslim Personal Law, Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939.
For Christian couples: Indian Divorce Act 1869 applies.

## CONSUMER PROTECTION ACT 2019
Forum jurisdiction based on claim value:
- District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission: Claims up to ₹1 crore
- State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission: Claims ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore
- National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission: Claims above ₹10 crore

How to file consumer complaint:
1. Send legal notice to seller/company first (recommended)
2. File complaint within 2 years of cause of action
3. Complaint must include: name/address of complainant, description of deficiency, relief sought
4. Pay prescribed court fees
5. Attach all evidence: purchase receipts, warranty cards, communication records

Online complaints can be filed at: edaakhil.nic.in

## PROPERTY LAW
Types of property disputes:
- Boundary disputes
- Title disputes
- Inheritance/succession disputes
- Landlord-tenant disputes
- Encroachment

Registration Act 1908: All immovable property transactions above ₹100 must be registered at Sub-Registrar's office.

Transfer of Property Act 1882: Governs sale, mortgage, lease, gift of property.

Limitation period to file property suit: Generally 12 years for possession; 3 years for money recovery.

## RTI (RIGHT TO INFORMATION ACT 2005)
Any citizen can request information from public authorities.

How to file RTI:
1. Write application to Public Information Officer (PIO) of the concerned department
2. Pay fee: ₹10 (Central Govt), varies for State Govt
3. Response must come within 30 days (48 hours for life/liberty matters)
4. First Appeal to Appellate Authority within 30 days if unsatisfied
5. Second Appeal to Central/State Information Commission within 90 days

## PIL (PUBLIC INTEREST LITIGATION)
A PIL can be filed by any citizen for matters of public interest.

Filed in:
- High Court under Article 226
- Supreme Court under Article 32

No court fees for PILs in most cases.
Court can take suo motu cognizance of public interest matters.

## LABOUR LAW
Key laws:
- Industrial Disputes Act 1947
- Factories Act 1948
- Minimum Wages Act 1948
- Payment of Gratuity Act 1972
- Employees' Provident Funds Act 1952
- POSH Act 2013 (Sexual harassment at workplace)

Grievance for wrongful termination:
1. File complaint with Labour Commissioner
2. Approach Labour Court
3. File writ petition in High Court if fundamental rights violated

## CYBER LAW (IT ACT 2000)
Common cyber offences:
- Section 66: Computer related offences (hacking) – 3 years imprisonment
- Section 66C: Identity theft – 3 years imprisonment + ₹1 lakh fine
- Section 66D: Cheating by impersonation using computer – 3 years + ₹1 lakh fine
- Section 67: Publishing obscene material online
- Section 72: Breach of confidentiality and privacy

File cybercrime complaint:
- Visit cybercrime.gov.in (National Cybercrime Reporting Portal)
- Visit local cyber crime cell
- File FIR at nearest police station

## LEGAL AID
Free legal aid is available to:
- Women and children
- Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes
- Persons with disabilities
- Victims of trafficking
- Persons in custody
- Persons with annual income below ₹1 lakh (varies by state)

Contact: District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) in your district.
National Legal Services Authority: nalsa.gov.in
