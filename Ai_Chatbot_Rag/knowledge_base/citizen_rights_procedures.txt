LEXASSIST KNOWLEDGE BASE – Legal Procedures & Citizen Rights
=============================================================

## FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (Constitution of India)

Article 14 – Right to Equality
All persons are equal before law and entitled to equal protection of laws.

Article 19 – Right to Freedom
Includes freedom of speech, expression, assembly, movement, profession.

Article 21 – Right to Life and Personal Liberty
No person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law.
This includes: right to live with dignity, right to education, right to health, right to privacy (Puttaswamy judgment 2017).

Article 22 – Protection Against Arrest and Detention
- Must be informed of grounds of arrest
- Right to consult a lawyer of your choice
- Must be produced before magistrate within 24 hours of arrest
- Cannot be detained beyond 24 hours without magistrate's order

Article 32 – Right to Constitutional Remedies
Right to move Supreme Court for enforcement of fundamental rights (Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called this the heart of Constitution).

## ARREST RIGHTS

What police MUST do when arresting you:
1. Inform you of the reason for arrest
2. Allow you to inform a relative or friend
3. Produce you before a magistrate within 24 hours (Section 57 CrPC)
4. Medical examination before and after custody
5. Memo of arrest signed by witness

What you have the RIGHT to do when arrested:
1. Remain silent (you cannot be forced to be a witness against yourself – Article 20)
2. Call a lawyer immediately
3. Call a family member
4. Know the charges against you
5. Apply for bail

D.K. Basu Guidelines (Supreme Court):
- Police must wear name tags
- Arrest memo must be prepared
- Time and place of interrogation must be recorded

## WOMEN'S LEGAL RIGHTS

Domestic Violence: Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005
- Includes physical, emotional, economic, sexual abuse
- File complaint with Protection Officer, Magistrate, or police station
- Can seek protection order, residence order, monetary relief

Sexual Harassment at Workplace (POSH Act 2013):
- Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) in organizations with 10+ employees
- Local Complaints Committee for unorganized sector
- Complaint must be filed within 3 months

Dowry Harassment (Section 498A IPC):
- Harassment for dowry demand is a non-bailable offence
- File FIR at any police station
- Can be filed by woman, her parents, relatives, or any person

Maternity Benefits:
- 26 weeks paid maternity leave for first two children (Maternity Benefit Act 2017)
- Available for organizations with 10+ employees

## PROPERTY RIGHTS

Hindu Succession Act 1956 (as amended 2005):
- Daughters have equal right in ancestral property as sons
- This applies even if father died before 2005 (Vineeta Sharma judgment 2020)
- Married women have equal rights in parental property

Tenant Rights (Rent Control Acts):
- Cannot be evicted without due legal process
- Landlord must give proper notice (usually 15-30 days for month-to-month tenancy)
- Rent cannot be increased arbitrarily (varies by state Rent Control Act)

Property Registration:
- Sale deed must be registered within 4 months of execution
- Registration fee: 1% of property value (varies by state)
- Stamp duty: Varies by state (3-10% of property value)

## LABOUR RIGHTS

Minimum Wages:
- Every worker entitled to minimum wage as notified by respective state government
- Complaint to Labour Commissioner if not paid

Gratuity (Payment of Gratuity Act 1972):
- Eligible after 5 years of continuous service
- Formula: (Last drawn salary × 15/26) × Number of years of service
- Payable within 30 days of leaving service

Provident Fund (EPF Act 1952):
- Both employer and employee contribute 12% each
- Can be claimed on retirement, resignation (after 2 months unemployment), or medical emergency

Wrongful Termination:
- Industrial Disputes Act protects workers in organizations with 100+ employees from retrenchment without government permission
- Others can file civil suit for breach of contract
- MSME sector workers can approach Labour Court

## CONSUMER RIGHTS

Six Consumer Rights:
1. Right to Safety
2. Right to be Informed
3. Right to Choose
4. Right to be Heard
5. Right to Redress
6. Right to Consumer Education

Filing Consumer Complaint:
1. Send written complaint/legal notice to company
2. File complaint at edaakhil.nic.in or visit Consumer Forum
3. Pay court fee (₹100-5000 depending on claim amount)
4. Attach: purchase documents, complaint copy, communication records
5. Forum may direct: refund, replacement, compensation, or punitive damages

Limitation period: 2 years from date of cause of action

## LEGAL AID & FREE SERVICES

National Legal Services Authority (NALSA):
- Website: nalsa.gov.in
- Toll-free: 15100
- Free legal services for eligible persons

Lok Adalat (People's Court):
- Alternative dispute resolution
- Settlement is final and binding
- No court fees, fast resolution
- Suitable for: motor accident claims, matrimonial disputes, labour disputes, land acquisition

Nyaya Mitra Scheme:
- Helps undertrials in getting bail and legal assistance

Tele Law Service:
- Common Service Centres (CSC) provide free legal advice via video conference
- Available in rural areas

## CYBER CRIME REPORTING

For online fraud, hacking, cyberbullying:
1. Visit cybercrime.gov.in
2. Call helpline: 1930 (National Cyber Crime Helpline)
3. Visit local Cyber Crime Cell

Categories handled online:
- Financial fraud / Online scams
- Social media crimes
- Child pornography / CSAM
- Ransomware
- Identity theft

Documents to keep:
- Screenshots of messages/transactions
- Bank statements
- Email headers
- URLs and usernames

## ENVIRONMENT AND PUBLIC INTEREST

National Green Tribunal (NGT):
- For environmental disputes
- Fast track court for environmental cases
- Citizens can file complaints for environmental violations

Public Grievances:
- CPGRAMS (Centralised Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System): pgportal.gov.in
- Prime Minister's Office: pmindia.gov.in
- State-level portals available in each state
