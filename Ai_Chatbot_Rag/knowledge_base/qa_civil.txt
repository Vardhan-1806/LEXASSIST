Q: What was the main legal issue in the case Vikash Kumar vs. Union Public Service Commission & Ors.?
A: The main legal issue was whether Vikash Kumar, who suffers from Writer's Cramp, was entitled to the assistance of a scribe during the Civil Services Examination under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016.
Case: Vikash Kumar vs. Union Public Service Commission & Ors.
Date: 11th February 2021

---

Q: Why did Vikash Kumar request a scribe for the Civil Services Examination?
A: Vikash Kumar requested a scribe because he suffers from Writer's Cramp, a neurological condition that severely affects his ability to write.
Case: Vikash Kumar vs. Union Public Service Commission & Ors.
Date: 11th February 2021

Q: What was the main legal issue in the case Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co. Ltd. vs. National Highways Authority of India (NHAI)?
A: The main legal issue was whether the Circular dated 15th February 2013 issued by the NHAI for the application of a new formula for price adjustment, which included a 'linking factor,' could be applied to the contract between Ssangyong Engineering and NHAI.
Case: Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co. Ltd. vs. National Highways Authority of India (NHAI)
Date: 8th May 2019

---

Q: What was the outcome of the arbitration between Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co. Ltd. and NHAI?
A: The arbitral tribunal, by a majority, held that the Circular could be applied as it was within the contractual stipulations, thus rejecting Ssangyong Engineering's claim. However, one member of the tribunal dissented, stating that the Circular was de hors the contract.
Case: Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co. Ltd. vs. National Highways Authority of India (NHAI)
Date: 8th May 2019

---

Q: What was the Supreme Court's decision regarding the application of the Circular and the arbitral award?
A: The Supreme Court set aside the majority arbitral award, stating that the introduction of the linking factor and the Circular were beyond the terms of the contract, thus constituting a decision on a matter beyond the scope of the submission to arbitration.
Case: Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co. Ltd. vs. National Highways Authority of India (NHAI)
Date: 8th May 2019

---

Q: What principle did the Supreme Court highlight regarding the interpretation of contracts in arbitration?
A: The Supreme Court highlighted that the arbitral tribunal must adhere strictly to the terms of the contract and cannot introduce new terms or apply extraneous documents, such as the Circular, which were not part of the original agreement between the parties.
Case: Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co. Ltd. vs. National Highways Authority of India (NHAI)
Date: 8th May 2019

---

Q: What was the Supreme Court's view on the use of government guidelines in the arbitral award?
A: The Supreme Court held that the use of government guidelines, which were not part of the contract, to determine the linking factor was inappropriate and constituted a jurisdictional error, leading to the setting aside of the arbitral award.
Case: Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co. Ltd. vs. National Highways Authority of India (NHAI)
Date: 8th May 2019

---

Q: What was the main legal issue in the case Shri Ram Shridhar Chimurkar vs. Union of India & Anr.?
A: The main legal issue was whether Shri Ram Shridhar Chimurkar, adopted by the widow of a deceased government servant after his death, was entitled to receive family pension under Rule 54(14)(b) of the Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1972.
Case: Shri Ram Shridhar Chimurkar vs. Union of India & Anr.
Date: 17th January 2023

Q: Why did the High Court conclude that time was not the essence of the contract in the sale agreements?
A: The High Court concluded that time was not the essence of the contract because the agreements did not stipulate a specific calendar date for the performance of the contract, and the vendors did not treat time as the essence of the contract through their conduct.
Case: Smt. Katta Sujatha Reddy & Anr. vs. Siddamsetty Infra Projects Pvt. Ltd. & Ors.
Date: 25th August 2022

---

Q: What was the Supreme Court's decision regarding the issue of limitation in this case?
A: The Supreme Court held that the suit for specific performance was barred by limitation as the purchaser failed to perform the contract within the stipulated time, and the limitation period had expired before the suit was filed.
Case: Smt. Katta Sujatha Reddy & Anr. vs. Siddamsetty Infra Projects Pvt. Ltd. & Ors.
Date: 25th August 2022

---

Q: What did the Supreme Court rule on the purchaser's readiness and willingness to perform the contract?
A: The Supreme Court found that the purchaser was not ready and willing to perform the contract as the purchaser failed to pay the balance sale consideration within the stipulated period and did not show any subsequent readiness and willingness to perform the contract.
Case: Smt. Katta Sujatha Reddy & Anr. vs. Siddamsetty Infra Projects Pvt. Ltd. & Ors.
Date: 25th August 2022

---

Q: What was the final ruling of the Supreme Court in this case?
A: The Supreme Court set aside the High Court's judgment, dismissed the suit for specific performance filed by Siddamsetty Infra Projects Pvt. Ltd., and held that the purchasers were not entitled to the relief of specific performance due to the suit being barred by limitation and lack of readiness and willingness to perform the contract.
Case: Smt. Katta Sujatha Reddy & Anr. vs. Siddamsetty Infra Projects Pvt. Ltd. & Ors.
Date: 25th August 2022

Q: What was the main legal issue in the case Garg Builders vs. Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited?

Case: Garg Builders vs. Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited

---

Q: What did Clause 17 of the contract between Garg Builders and Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited stipulate?
A: Clause 17 of the contract stipulated that no interest shall be payable by BHEL on Earnest Money Deposit, Security Deposit, or on any moneys due to the contractor.
Case: Garg Builders vs. Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited

Q: What was the Arbitrator's decision regarding pendente lite interest?
A: The Arbitrator awarded pendente lite interest at the rate of 10% per annum to Garg Builders on the award amount from the date of filing of the claim petition until the realization of the award amount, stating there was no prohibition in the contract regarding such interest.
Case: Garg Builders vs. Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited

---

Q: How did the Delhi High Court rule on the challenge to the Arbitrator's award?
A: The Delhi High Court, both the Single Judge and Division Bench, set aside the Arbitrator's award regarding pendente lite interest, stating that Clause 17 of the contract expressly barred such interest and the Arbitrator had exceeded his jurisdiction.
Case: Garg Builders vs. Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited

Q: What was the Supreme Court's stance on the arbitrator's power to award pendente lite interest under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996?
A: The Supreme Court held that under Section 31(7)(a) of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, if the contract expressly bars payment of interest, the Arbitrator does not have the power to award pendente lite interest.
Case: Garg Builders vs. Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited

Q: What was the compensation amount awarded by the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal?
A: The Tribunal awarded a total compensation of Rs.65,60,347.20 to the appellants, which included amounts for loss of dependency, loss of consortium, loss of love and affection, and funeral expenses.

Q: On what grounds did the High Court reduce the compensation awarded by the Tribunal?

Q: What was the Supreme Court's final decision regarding the compensation awarded?

Q: How did the Supreme Court address the issue of compensation for loss of consortium and love and affection?
A: The Supreme Court noted that while the High Court found a double award for loss of consortium and love and affection, it did not justify reducing the total compensation. The Tribunal's award was seen as appropriate in its totality.

Q: What wasthe primary issue in the case between Director General, Doordarshan Prasar Bharti Corporation of India & Anr. and Smt. Magi H Desai?
A: The primary issue was whether the services rendered by Smt. Magi H Desai as a contractual/casual employee from 1985 to 31.03.1995 should be counted as temporary service for the purpose of calculating qualifying service for pensionary/retiral benefits.
Case: Director General, Doordarshan Prasar Bharti Corporation of India & Anr. vs. Smt. Magi H Desai

---

Q: What was the decision of the High Court regarding the service period of Smt. Magi H Desai?
A: The High Court directed that the services rendered by Smt. Magi H Desai as a contractual/casual employee should be counted as temporary service for the purpose of calculating qualifying service for pensionary/retiral benefits.
Case: Director General, Doordarshan Prasar Bharti Corporation of India & Anr. vs. Smt. Magi H Desai

---

Q: What was the contention of the appellants, Doordarshan Prasar Bharti Corporation of India, regarding the High Court's decision?
A: The appellants contended that the services rendered by Smt. Magi H Desai as a casual/contractual employee could not be considered as temporary service for the purpose of pensionary/retiral benefits under Rule 13 of the Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1972.
Case: Director General, Doordarshan Prasar Bharti Corporation of India & Anr. vs. Smt. Magi H Desai

---

Q: What was the Supreme Court's interpretation of Rule 13 of the Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1972 in this case?
A: The Supreme Court interpreted Rule 13 to mean that only services rendered in an officiating or temporary capacity, followed by substantive appointment, qualify for pensionary benefits. Services rendered as casual/contractual do not qualify under this rule.
Case: Director General, Doordarshan Prasar Bharti Corporation of India & Anr. vs. Smt. Magi H Desai

---

Q: How did the Supreme Court rule regarding the High Court's judgment?
A: The Supreme Court quashed and set aside the High Court's judgment, ruling that the services rendered by Smt. Magi H Desai as a casual/contractual employee could not be counted for pensionary/retiral benefits.
Case: Director General, Doordarshan Prasar Bharti Corporation of India & Anr. vs. Smt. Magi H Desai

---

Q: What was the final outcome of the Supreme Court's decision in this case?
A: The Supreme Court restored the judgment of the Central Administrative Tribunal, which dismissed the Original Application filed by Smt. Magi H Desai, and ruled that her services as a casual/contractual employee could not be counted for pensionary/retiral benefits.
Case: Director General, Doordarshan Prasar Bharti Corporation of India & Anr. vs. Smt. Magi H Desai

Q: What was the primary legal issue in the case of Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Co. Ltd. vs. M/s. JSW Steel Limited & Ors.?

Case: Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Co. Ltd. vs. M/s. JSW Steel Limited & Ors.

---

Q: What did the State Commission initially decide regarding the levy of additional surcharge on captive consumers?
A: The State Commission decided that the additional surcharge under Section 42(4) of the Electricity Act, 2003 is leviable on captive consumers/captive users.
Case: Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Co. Ltd. vs. M/s. JSW Steel Limited & Ors.

---

Q: What was the decision of the Appellate Tribunal for Electricity, Delhi, regarding the additional surcharge on captive consumers?
A: The Appellate Tribunal for Electricity set aside the State Commission's order and held that the group of captive consumers are not liable to pay additional surcharge under Section 42(4) of the Electricity Act, 2003.
Case: Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Co. Ltd. vs. M/s. JSW Steel Limited & Ors.

---

Q: How did the Supreme Court interpret the applicability of Section 42(4) to captive consumers?
A: The Supreme Court interpreted that Section 42(4) is applicable only when the State Commission permits consumers to receive electricity from sources other than their distribution licensee, which does not apply to captive consumers who have statutory rights under Section 9.
Case: Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Co. Ltd. vs. M/s. JSW Steel Limited & Ors.

---

Q: What was the Supreme Court's final decision regarding the additional surcharge on captive consumers?
A: The Supreme Court upheld the decision of the Appellate Tribunal, ruling that captive consumers are not liable to pay the additional surcharge under Section 42(4) of the Electricity Act, 2003.
Case: Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Co. Ltd. vs. M/s. JSW Steel Limited & Ors.

---

Q: What direction did the Supreme Court give regarding the additional surcharge already recovered from captive consumers?
