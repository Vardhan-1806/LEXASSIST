This was seen as a threat to the traditional authority of Indian judges, including father-son judges, who were mostly Brahmins with generations of legal experience. The bill sparked widespread protests in India and political pressure in Britain, leading to its amendment and eventual passing in a modified form. However, the controversy highlighted the tensions between the colonial administration and the Indian legal elite, including father-son judges, and contributed to the broader movement for Indian self-rule.

Q: How has the debate on taxation evolved from ancient times to the modern era, particularly in terms of its application and impact on governance and societal welfare?
A: The debate on taxation has evolved from ancient times to the modern era, broadening in dimensions and focusing more on its application and impact on governance and societal welfare. In ancient times, taxation was seen as a responsibility of the king to provide for the common good and basic amenities. For instance, six percent income tax is described in Kalidasa's Abhignanshakuntalam.
